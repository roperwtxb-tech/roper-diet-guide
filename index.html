Restaurant cheat sheet. A practical guide for eating out safely across all four diet rules. It's okay to tell staff this is a medical necessity for a child, not a preference — most kitchens take that seriously and will work with you.
🗣
Before You Go & What to Say
Planning ahead and talking to staff

Plan ahead

  • Look at the menu online first and call during a quiet time (mid-afternoon, not peak dinner rush) to ask questions.
  • Favor restaurants built around simple grilled or roasted protein and vegetables over anything sauce-heavy or fried.
  • Bring this page (or a screenshot) to hand to the server or kitchen if it helps.

What to say

  • Tell the server it's a medical necessity, not a preference — staff are usually more careful and more willing to check with the kitchen.
  • Ask for the simplest possible version: protein cooked plain, vegetables steamed or roasted with nothing added, sauces and dressings left off or served on the side.
  • Ask specifically: is the fryer shared with breaded food? Is a separate pan or fresh oil used? Does the sauce or seasoning contain flour, soy sauce, vinegar, or added sugar?
  • Once the food arrives, confirm with the server that it was made exactly as requested before eating.

🚨
Universal Red Flags
Ask about these at every restaurant, any cuisine
  • Soy sauce & teriyaki — brewed with wheat (gluten) and fermented (high histamine). Hides in marinades, stir-fries, fried rice, and glazes.
  • Vinegar & vinegar-based sauces — ketchup, mustard, mayonnaise, BBQ sauce, ranch, most salad dressings. All fermented, all high histamine.
  • Aged, cured, or smoked foods — aged cheese, deli meat, bacon, sausage, smoked fish. Among the highest histamine foods there are.
  • Anything that's been sitting or reheated — soup of the day, slow-simmered sauces, stews made hours earlier. Histamine climbs the longer food sits, even after cooking.
  • Shared fryer oil or shared grill — if breaded or battered food is cooked in the same oil or surface, that's a gluten cross-contact risk.
  • Pre-made marinades and sauces in general — usually built on sugar, vinegar, or soy sauce even when it isn't obvious.
  • Artificial food dyes (Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Red 40, Red 3) — turn up in sauces, drink mixes, and kids' menu items. Some children react with skin or behavior changes.
  • MSG / "flavor enhancer" — common in fried rice, broths, and seasoning blends, especially at Asian-American and fast food chains.
  • Sulfites — dried fruit, some salad bars, and vinegar-based sauces.

Safe Bets, Almost Anywhere
A go-to order that works at most restaurants
  • Plain grilled, baked, or roasted protein with nothing added — chicken, steak, a burger patty with no bun, or very fresh fish.
  • Steamed or roasted plain vegetables — ask for olive oil, ghee, or butter instead of a butter substitute or seed oil.
  • A side salad with oil and salt/pepper only, no dressing (dressings are almost always vinegar-based).
  • A plain baked potato, or ask to swap starch for extra vegetables.
  • Ask for any sauce "on the side" or left off entirely so you control what touches the food.
🥩
Steakhouse / Brazilian Steakhouse
Best-fit cuisine — built around plain grilled meat

The easiest cuisine for this combination of diets. The meal is built around meat seasoned with just salt and fire, so there's very little to hide.

Order

  • Meat seasoned with salt only, no marinade, no butter baste or glaze.

Watch for

  • Shared salad bar items — cured meats, cheese, and vinegar dressings often sit right next to safe options.
🌯
Mexican
Good, with a few swaps

Corn tortillas, plain grilled meats (carne asada, pollo asado), and rice give a naturally gluten-free base.

Order

  • Meat grilled plain, corn tortillas (confirm corn only, not a corn/flour blend), plain rice.

Watch for

  • Flour tortillas, shredded cheese, sour cream, tomato-based salsas (histamine), and canned refried beans, which often carry additives.
🍜
Thai / Vietnamese
Good, with a few swaps

Rice noodles, rice dishes, fresh herbs, and grilled meats are a solid starting point.

Order

  • Ask for the dish made without soy sauce or fish sauce, with any sauce on the side.

Watch for

  • Fish sauce and soy sauce turn up in almost everything, along with peanut sauce, tamarind, and MSG in broths.
🍏
Indian
Good, with a few swaps

Tandoori-grilled meats and plain rice can work well.

Order

  • Meat grilled plain, without the yogurt marinade, and dishes without a cream sauce.

Watch for

  • Yogurt marinades (dairy plus fermentation), heavy cream sauces, and chutneys, which are usually vinegar or fruit-based and fermented.
🍣
Japanese / Sushi
Highest risk — needs extra care or best skipped

This is the hardest cuisine for this combination of diets, for two separate reasons: traditional soy sauce is brewed with wheat, and sushi rice vinegar is sometimes seasoned with a wheat-based product — both a gluten problem. Separately, raw and aged fish is one of the highest histamine-risk foods there is, since histamine builds the longer fish sits after being caught.

If you go anyway

  • Ask specifically for tamari (gluten-free soy sauce) and a cooked option made with fish prepared that day, not sitting in a case.

My Japanese World, Is Soy Sauce Gluten-Free; Endless Distances, Gluten Free Japan Guide

🍕
Italian
Risky — needs a simplified order

Pasta is the obvious problem, but tomato sauce simmered for hours, aged parmesan, cured meats (prosciutto, salami), and balsamic vinegar are just as much of a concern.

Order

  • A simply grilled protein with olive oil and a plain vegetable side. Skip the pasta, red sauce, and cheese entirely.
🍔
American Diner / Grill / BBQ
Doable with a plain order

Order

  • A plain burger patty (no bun, no cheese), grilled chicken, plain eggs, or steamed vegetables tend to work.

Watch for

  • BBQ sauce (sugar, vinegar, and often gluten), pre-seasoned patties, a shared griddle with buns, bacon, deli meat, and coleslaw (vinegar-based dressing).
🍟
Fast Food
Generally best avoided

Most fast food relies on batter, shared fryers, pre-made sauces, and additives like MSG and food dyes — several diet rules get broken at once. If it's unavoidable, a plain grilled item with no bun and no sauce is the least-risky option, but this should be the rare exception, not a routine choice.
